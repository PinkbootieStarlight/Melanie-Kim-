# Perfect competition (farm) — engagement brief

## The problem

Creating a farm plan growing tomatoes, carrots and mesclun to maximize profit.
The plan would determine how many beds to grow for each crop, and how many
temp workers to hire (up to 4 total temp workers).

These are the constraints: market price of each crop, season length of 36
weeks, total land of 1.5 acres, $20,000 fixed costs, farmer's committed work
hours, total number of beds ≤ 64, and total labor hours available (farmer's
hours and work hours from up to 4 temp workers).

## What I am assuming

I assume prices remain unchanged all season. I assume that the farm lab
calculator is close to actual yields, and that the diminishing-returns
compounding and farmer/temp-worker wage split accurately reflect labor
costs — reducing profit as beds scale up.

## Hypothesis

I expect to grow 9 tomato beds, 11 carrot beds, 29 mesclun beds, because
carrot's marginal profit is higher than tomatoes, which is costlier with
fertilizer expenses and more labor intensive than the other 2 crops.

## How I would know I was wrong

My plan is assuming that I plant less than 20 beds of tomatoes and
carrots each, and less than 30 beds of mesclun, but this hypothesis may not be
true as I may still grow additional crops like carrots and make a profit.
