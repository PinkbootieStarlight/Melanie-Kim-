# Perfect Competition Farm Allocation — Recommendation Memo

## Recommendation

The farm should allocate **10 beds to tomatoes, 20 beds to carrots, and 30 beds to mesclun**. This 60-bed allocation maximizes season profit under the model's production, labor, land, and crop constraints.

The Solver results show that the **carrot cap of 20 beds and mesclun cap of 30 beds are binding**, while the tomato cap, total 64-bed capacity, available land, and temporary-worker limit remain slack. Therefore, maximizing profit does not require using all 64 available beds or all 1.5 acres.

## Economic Rationale

Marginal analysis explains why the optimal allocation stops at 10 tomato beds. Tomato marginal profit remains positive through bed 10, but the next bed has marginal cost greater than marginal revenue. Adding that bed would therefore reduce profit.

The analysis also changed my initial assessment of the farm plan. My original hypothesis of **9 tomato, 11 carrot, and 29 mesclun beds** was more conservative because I focused heavily on the rising costs of expanding production. Comparing marginal revenue with marginal cost showed that additional production can remain profitable even as total variable costs increase.

## Decision

I recommend the **10 tomato / 20 carrot / 30 mesclun** allocation. The remaining four-bed capacity and unused land should not be viewed as inefficiency. Under the model, expanding production beyond the profit-maximizing allocation would not improve the farm's economic outcome.
